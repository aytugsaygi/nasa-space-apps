# Impact

**The problem.** On Mars, a message to Earth takes about 3 minutes at closest approach and 22.5 minutes at the farthest, one way [NASA, Houston We Have a Podcast Ep. 59]. Help from the ground cannot be instant, so astronauts must understand and manage their own health.

**Why it matters.** About 70% of long-duration crew show the earliest signs of eye changes (SANS) [NASA HRP Evidence Report]. Astronauts on the station sleep 6.09 hours on average, against 8.5 hours scheduled [Barger et al., The Lancet Neurology]. Without exercise, bones lose 1–1.5% of their mineral density a month [NASA, The Human Body in Space; NASA HRP Evidence Report on bone fracture].

**Who can use it today.** NASA runs analog missions on Earth: the HERA habitat (650 square feet), and CHAPEA, where a crew of four lives in the 1,700-square-foot Mars Dune Alpha for a year [NASA HRP, Hazard: Isolation and Confinement]. NASA also studies analog populations "like submariners and people who live in Antarctica" [NASA, Houston We Have a Podcast Ep. 59]. Health Atlas works offline and explains every alert with its source, so it fits these crews.

**Why openness matters.** Every component is openly licensed, so agencies and research teams could reuse and extend it.
